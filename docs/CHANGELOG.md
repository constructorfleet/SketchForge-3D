# Changelog

- Sketch constraints now include a perpendicular relation between two selected segments. The solver projects both segments toward a right angle during point edits, and the constraint persists in `.skf` sketches and is represented in the TypeScript client profile type.

## Unreleased

- A face draft or prism draft made in the editor on a sketch extrusion is now kept as a feature instead of being baked. The editor writes the same record the REST API does (`faceDraft`, `faceDraftSourceFaceIds`, `faceDraftSourceSegmentId` and `faceDraftSourceFrame` for a face draft; `draftPrismAngle`, `draftPrismSourceHeight` and `draftPrismSourceFrame` for a prism draft), and places the part where the REST API does, so one record means one placement whichever side wrote it. Both sides now call one engine implementation to find the drafted face (including the fallback for drafts saved before sketch segments were recorded), to draft it, and to build the record. A new **Face draft** card in the shape inspector changes the draft's direction and angle afterwards, and the **Draft prism** card shows the kept angle with an **Update draft** button. Editing the sketch replays the draft on the new profile. The edit is refused with a message, and the sketch left open, when the draft cannot be rebuilt on the new profile, or when the shape was mirrored, resized, filleted or chamfered after it was drafted, since replaying would undo that. A second face draft, a prism draft on a face-drafted shape, and a face draft on a prism-drafted shape are refused, as the REST API refuses them, instead of being baked over the kept draft. The draft survives `.skf` round trips, and undo steps back through each change and then the draft itself. Drafts are kept on single-loop sketch extrusions that are exact, untreated, unmirrored and not resized, on a flat face that maps to one side of the profile or to a cap. Any other shape, and a face the record cannot name, keeps the baked draft and its existing notice; a baked draft now also clears any stale draft record. Face colours are cleared by a draft, as before; the plain-revolve case in #356 is unchanged. The new messages are in English only for now, like the rest of the **Faces** panel.
- Repeated face drafts now refuse kernel results whose volume or bounds grow far beyond the input solid, preventing a bad draft from stretching a face across the workspace. The Faces panel also explains that drafting can change face numbers and points to the current Face ID.

- Faces can now be picked in the viewport. While the selected shape's **Faces** panel is open, the face under the pointer is highlighted, and clicking it chooses it for colouring or drafting, so the face stepper no longer has to be walked to find one. Dragging the shape, Shift-click, the transform handles and the workplane, ruler, edge-modifier and align modes are unchanged; those modes and camera moves clear the hover highlight, and a press that moves the shape does not also change the chosen face. The stepper stays as the keyboard way to choose a face. A shape whose faces have stable IDs now shows the chosen face's ID in the panel, the same ID `GET /faces` lists for it. The hover highlight is drawn as a separate overlay, so moving across faces does not rebuild the shape. Picking only changes the highlight and the panel's chosen face: nothing is stored and no undo step is recorded.
- Fixed rolled round cylinders and cones taking a different yaw in mesh booleans and exports than they show in the viewport. Tilted round meshes now keep the full yaw; untilted meshes retain their facet-aligned yaw snapping.
- Fixed exact torus solids being centred at the workplane instead of resting on it. Face editing and exact cuts now keep an untilted or tilted torus aligned with the viewport.

- A shape tilted about a horizontal axis (**Tilt X** or **Tilt Z**) is now built exactly where the viewport draws it. Its exact solid was turned about the shape's base instead of its centre, so it landed a little off: a 30 × 10 × 6 mm box tilted 20° sat 0.18 mm low and 1.03 mm forward. That moved an exact cut made with a tilted cutter, or into a tilted solid, by the same amount. It also moved the edge midpoints and face centroids that `GET /edges` and `GET /faces` report for a tilted shape, which now turn about its centre too. Turning about the vertical axis was never affected. **Enable face editing** is now offered for tilted shapes as well.
- Fixed a fillet, chamfer or hollow of a mirrored shape coming back unmirrored. The result is stored as geometry with the shape's mirror flags cleared, but the server treated the solid without applying them first, so the mirror was lost and the operation still reported success. This hit shapes stored as geometry (imported, revolved, or already treated) and parametric shapes alike. The mirror is now baked into the solid before it is treated, the way the editor draws it: in the shape's own frame, before its rotation. `GET /edges` and `GET /faces` list the mirrored solid too, so an edge or face index names the one you see.
- Face colours and face drafts now share one **Faces** panel in the shape inspector, styled like the other property cards. A box, cylinder, sphere or other parametric solid offers **Enable face editing**, which rebuilds it as exact geometry in place, so a fillet or chamfer is no longer needed first. Imported pictures and shapes tilted about a horizontal axis do not offer it yet. One face stepper, numbered from 1 and showing the face count, drives both colour and draft, and the chosen face stays outlined in the view while the panel is open, even when it already has a color. Coloured faces are listed with a swatch and can be selected or cleared from the list. The panel no longer shows API routes or kernel terms. The draft's pull axis is now labelled **Direction**, and its error messages say what to try next.
- Face colours now show in the viewport on shapes made exact in the editor: filleted or chamfered shapes, sketch extrusions and sweeps, draft prisms and drafted faces. The editor's CAD workers meshed these without saying which triangles belong to which face, so a stored colour had nothing to paint until export. Server-side CAD results had the opposite fault. They stored each face's range in index-buffer entries rather than triangles, so a colour covered three times the triangles it should. New results store triangles, and the viewport recognises and converts ranges stored the old way.
- Face colours chosen in the shape inspector are now kept. The editor treated a change that touched only a shape's face colours as no change and dropped it, so the card kept saying "No face colors assigned." The same check ignored 13 other fields, including face IDs, face and prism drafts, shell settings and STEP assembly links. It now compares every field a shape carries, so a field added later cannot be skipped the same way.
- The TypeScript client can now cut, group, ungroup and separate shapes. `client.booleans.cut(projectId, { solidIds, holeIds })`, `client.groups.group(projectId, ids)`, `client.groups.ungroup(projectId, shapeId)` and `client.parts.separate(projectId, shapeId)` call `POST /booleans`, `POST /groups`, `DELETE /groups` and `POST /parts`. The project handle has the same four as `project.cut`, `group`, `ungroup` and `separate`, matching the Python client. Each is retried when the project lock is busy. A new example macro, `tap-drill-plate.macro.mjs`, drills a plate with `project.cut` and exports it as STL entirely through the API, and runs in the package integration suite. The README no longer claims the API has no grouping endpoint. Instead it records the real limit: a `thread` cutter is stored as a mesh, and the server's boolean cannot cut it yet.
- The TypeScript client can now export a project to every format the API produces. `client.exports.export(projectId, { format, shapeIds?, name?, view?, section? })` and `project.export(...)` return the file as a `Uint8Array` for `stl`, `obj`, `3mf`, `step`, `skf`, `zip`, `svg` and `dxf`, with STEP's `exportedCount` and `skipped` shapes. A `view` on anything but SVG, or a `section` on anything but SVG or DXF, is refused before the request is sent. `project.estimateWallThickness(shapeId)`, which the package README already documented, now exists.
- Fillets, selected-edge treatments, and `GET /edges` on a revolve now use its
  exact solid instead of its tessellated mesh. A revolve carries no exact
  B-rep of its own, so the server sewed its mesh into a solid: a 24-sided ring
  listed 288 facet edges, all filletable, and a fillet "succeeded" by rounding
  every facet (7568 triangles in 2.3 s). An untreated plain full-turn revolve
  is now rebuilt exactly from its profile for the edge listing, edge
  measurements, and every fillet or chamfer, so that ring lists its four
  corner circles (and two non-filletable seams), a fillet blends them with
  tori, and edge indexes passed back to `POST /treatments` name those circles.
  A resized revolve is rebuilt at its drawn size. A source edit replays a
  revolve fillet against the exact topology. Partial-turn, helical, cored,
  multi-region, and unevenly stretched revolves have no exact solid, so their
  treatments and edge listings are refused with `422` naming the limitation
  instead of working on facets. A second treatment on an already treated
  revolve now builds on its stored geometry; an all-edge chamfer used to
  rebuild the revolve from its profile and discard the earlier treatment.
- Fixed a sketch source edit inside a turned, mirrored or resized boolean
  group moving the group's part. Recompute added the rebuilt part's offset to
  the group's position as if the group were unturned, dropped the group's
  mirror flags, and snapped a group resized after creation back to its natural
  size. The rebuilt group is now placed through the group's own frame -- the
  same resize, mirror and turn that ungrouping applies to its children -- and
  keeps its `rotation`, `rotationX`, `rotationZ`, mirror flags and its
  `width`/`depth`/`height` to `groupedBase*` ratios.
- Security: updating a git-checkout install from the dashboard now requires `SKETCHFORGE_UPDATE_ADMIN_KEY` (entered in the update dialog) or an explicit `SKETCHFORGE_LOCAL_UPDATE=1`. Previously, `POST /api/app-update` ran `git fetch`, `git merge`, and `npm install` with no key for any request addressed to `localhost`. That test reads the Host header, so any machine on the network could trigger it. With neither setting, the dialog links to the update guide and the endpoint returns 403. Docker deployments and the desktop app are unaffected. See [application updates](guides/deployment.md#application-updates).
- Editor keyboard shortcuts no longer act on the model while Workspace Settings, or any other modal dialog, is open. Previously Backspace or Delete with focus on a button in Settings deleted the selection underneath, and `h`, `s`, `m`, `d` and the view keys changed the model or camera. Escape now closes Workspace Settings (and leaves the selection alone); Escape inside its grid colour picker still closes only the picker.
- Editing the sketch behind a filleted or chamfered extrusion, sweep, or
  revolve through the REST API or MCP no longer silently drops the treatment.
  `POST /treatments` now records every fillet and chamfer on the shape, as the
  editor already did, so a source edit replays one all-edge fillet or
  symmetric 45° all-edge chamfer on the rebuilt solid. A source edit after a
  selected-edge treatment, a non-45° chamfer, more than one treatment, or a
  shell is refused with `422` and writes nothing, where it used to return the
  bare solid. Because API treatments are now recorded, the existing refusals
  for shelling or adding a prism draft after a treatment apply to them too.
- The Docker container now prepares its storage directories when it starts: an entrypoint creates whatever `SKETCHFORGE_SHARED_PROJECTS_DIR` and `SKETCHFORGE_PROJECTS_DIR` name, hands each directory to the `node` user, and then drops privileges before the server runs. Saving to a fresh volume at `/data/my-projects`, at a path of your own, or to a volume left root-owned by an earlier image no longer fails. Started without root, the container logs which configured directory it cannot write instead of failing on the first save. The container health check no longer reports unhealthy when shared projects are turned off. See [volume ownership](guides/deployment.md#volume-ownership).
- The GitHub Pages documentation workflow now publishes a browser-only SketchForge editor demo at `/SketchForge-3D/Demo/`, with subpath-aware static assets and a direct navigation link.
- README now has a dedicated installation overview for the browser app, desktop releases, Docker, source checkouts, and static exports. The documentation site's top bar groups guides and references into dropdowns and links directly to installation.
- The local MCP now exposes `sketchforge_import_stl`, which imports an STL file from the MCP host filesystem into a chosen local project.
- Retained face drafts on straight-line, single-region, no-hole sketch extrusion
  side faces now follow the same sketch segment through source-profile edits,
  even when the face normal changes. The segment identity survives `.skf`
  round trips; missing or ambiguous segment matches fail without writing.
- An untreated face draft on a single-loop sketch extrusion can now be
  reselected by an original source-face ID that remains uniquely planar in the
  current output. The allowlist persists in `.skf`; generated IDs, ambiguous or
  missing IDs, treated shapes, and profile edits combined with reselection are
  refused atomically. Profile edits refresh IDs from rebuilt source topology
  without transferring IDs by CAD face index.
- A retained face draft on a plain single-region full-turn sketch revolve can
  now change its angle while preserving one all-edge fillet or symmetric 45°
  all-edge chamfer. The engine replays revolve, draft, then treatment atomically,
  preserving output/source/source-face IDs and refreshing treatment history and
  eligible-edge counts. Angle-only, pull-axis-only, and combined angle/axis
  edits preserve output/source/source-face IDs. `.skf` round trips and
  subsequent profile or revolve-setting edits preserve the sequence; failed
  replays roll back without writing. Multi-region, partial-turn, and helical
  revolves remain refused.
- A retained face draft on a single-section, hole-free sketch sweep can now
  change its angle, signed pull axis, or both together while preserving one
  all-edge fillet or symmetric 45° chamfer. Recompute applies source, draft,
  then treatment atomically; `.skf` round trips and subsequent profile edits
  preserve the updated draft, treatment, and IDs. Source-face reselection and
  unsupported sweep cases remain refused.
- A retained face draft on a single-loop sketch extrusion can now change its
  angle while preserving one all-edge fillet or symmetric 45° chamfer. The
  engine replays source, draft, then treatment atomically and refreshes
  treatment history; `.skf` round trips and source-profile edits preserve the
  sequence. Signed pull-axis-only and combined angle/axis edits are also
  supported; invalid axes or failed replays roll back without writing.
- A face-drafted single-loop sketch extrusion can now retain one symmetric 45°
  all-edge chamfer. `.skf` round trips preserve the sequence, and source-profile
  edits replay the extrusion, face draft, then chamfer atomically while
  refreshing treatment history and eligible-edge counts.
- A face-drafted single-section, hole-free sketch sweep can now retain one
  symmetric 45° all-edge chamfer. `.skf` round trips preserve the treatment,
  and source-profile edits replay the sweep, face draft, then chamfer atomically.
- `POST /imports` now documents and validates both base64 JSON and multipart
  uploads, its shape/triangle-count/revision response, and reachable JSON
  errors. The generated OpenAPI subset covers 26 of 32 operations; 6 remain
  omitted.
- `POST /patterns` now validates normalized IDs, defaults, integral count limits,
  finite numeric options, its 201 shape-summary response, and reachable errors.
  The generated OpenAPI subset covers 26 of 32 registered operations; 6 remain
  omitted.
- `POST /explode` now validates trimmed project and shape query IDs, returned
  STEP assembly shape summaries and revision, and reachable errors. The
  generated contract includes its request parameters and response schemas.
- `POST /mirrors` now validates normalized shape IDs, principal-axis selection,
  shape-summary responses, and reachable error responses at the route boundary.
- A sketch extrusion with a retained prism draft can now take one all-edge fillet or symmetric 45° chamfer. Source-profile and draft-angle edits rebuild the extrusion, reapply its prism draft, then replay the treatment; attempts to add the draft after a treatment remain refused.
- `PATCH /shapes` can retain one face draft on a sketch extrusion, single-solid sketch sweep, or plain single-region full-turn sketch revolve through the `faceDraft` value. For eligible revolves, `GET /faces?topology=exact` lists analytic B-rep faces; the default mesh listing and its compatibility indexes remain unchanged. The selected source face is referenced by stable face ID and uniquely matched planar normal during source-profile edits; `.skf` round trips preserve the draft, its source frame, and a resized revolve's scale. Ambiguous or missing matches and ineffective dependent booleans fail atomically. Partial, helical, and multi-region revolve drafts are refused. A plain single-region full-turn sketch revolve can take one symmetric 45° all-edge chamfer or one all-edge fillet after the face draft; a single-loop extrusion can take one all-edge fillet; and a single-section, hole-free sweep can take one all-edge fillet or one symmetric 45° all-edge chamfer. Source-profile edits replay each supported source, draft, then treatment atomically. A retained prism draft on a sketch extrusion can be followed by one all-edge fillet or symmetric 45° chamfer; adding a prism draft after a treatment remains refused, and shelling is refused when persisted draft or treatment metadata exists. The editor's arbitrary-shape draft remains baked.
- A face-drafted single-loop sketch extrusion can now take one all-edge fillet. Source-profile edits replay the draft before the fillet; `.skf` round trips preserve the feature sequence and treatment history. Chamfers and other face-draft/treatment combinations remain refused.
- `POST /groups` and `DELETE /groups` now have runtime-validated request and
  response contracts in generated OpenAPI, including shape summaries and
  validation/engine error responses.
- `POST /api/v1/parts` now has a runtime-validated contract for trimmed target
  IDs, split shape summaries, and documented refusal responses.
- Editing the source profile of a retained sketch extrusion, sweep, or revolve now replays one persisted all-edge fillet or symmetric 45° all-edge chamfer against rebuilt topology instead of carrying stale edge indexes. The engine refreshes treatment history and eligible-edge counts; revolve chamfers require a plain full turn with one closed profile region. Revolve setting edits also replay treatments, and the change is rejected without writing the project if the new geometry cannot accept them. Selected-edge treatments, non-45° chamfers, multiple treatment steps, adding a prism draft after a treatment, and draft/treatment combinations outside the retained prism-draft-first single-treatment path remain unsupported.
- Sketch constraints can now keep two selected segments parallel. The bounded solver projects their directions during point edits, the inspector includes the relation in its local degree-of-freedom estimate, and `.skf` persistence plus the TypeScript sketch-profile type retain it. Parallel does not constrain the segments' offset; broader constraint solving remains local and bounded.
- `GET /thickness` and `POST /interference` have runtime-validated request and
  response contracts in the generated OpenAPI reference. Interference describes
  its bounded exact/tessellated report and 400/422 validation and limit errors.
  The documented subset now covers 19 of 32 registered operations; 13 remain
  omitted.
- Exploding a STEP assembly now creates editable nested groups that follow its product tree when every solid has an unambiguous occurrence association. The exact solid geometry and project-space positions stay on the leaves; moving a group moves its subassembly, and ungrouping works one level at a time. If any association is missing or ambiguous, explosion keeps the existing flat result so no solid disappears. Native STEP transform operations and hierarchy-preserving STEP export remain unsupported.
- Editing a sketch-backed extrusion, revolve, or sweep through its persisted `sketchSourceId` rebuilds its geometry and recomputes retained boolean ancestors bottom-up in one project write. A revolve source edit can also change operation settings such as `sides`. The source and output IDs and placement remain stable; invalid profiles or dependent booleans that fail leave the project unchanged. Other downstream operation types remain baked. See the [engine package guide](./packages/engine.md).
- Prism drafts on sketch extrusions now retain their angle and original extrusion frame/height. `changeShape` can update the draft angle by `sketchSourceId`, and profile edits reapply the draft before recomputing retained boolean ancestors. Invalid draft edits or ineffective dependent booleans leave the project unchanged; face drafts and other downstream operations remain baked.
- The configuration inventory now covers supported server/container, TypeScript/Python client, and local MCP runtime settings. Each setting links to all verified readers and owner guides; build, test, CI, desktop development, and pass-through environment values remain classified outside the supported runtime inventory.

- The generated OpenAPI contract now includes both measurement routes, with
  runtime validation for conditional edge and face indices and the exclusive
  angle result fields. The subset documents nine of 32 registered operations.
- The generated OpenAPI contract now includes `POST /align` and
  `POST /distribute`, with runtime request and response validation for the
  shape-arrangement operations. The subset documents eleven of 32 registered
  operations.
- The generated OpenAPI contract now includes server-side booleans, edge
  treatments, shelling, and draft-prism operations, with runtime request and
  response validation. The subset documents fifteen of 32 registered
  operations.
- Shape parameter edits now keep face IDs and colors when a face has a unique
  counterpart with the same surface type and outward normal. A uniquely
  occurring cone, sphere, torus, or cylinder surface also retains identity when
  its sampled normal changes; repeated curved surfaces remain unmatched.
- 3MF export converts OCCT face group index ranges to triangle ranges correctly,
  so color assignments on any face are attached to their triangles.
- The REST API and TypeScript/Python clients can create sketch sweeps from closed section paths and one open spine. `PATCH /shapes` accepts a replacement `sketchProfile` and rebuilds the OCCT solid while preserving output/source identities and resize ratios. Face colors are cleared and face IDs regenerated for the new topology.
- The documentation site renders ```mermaid fences as diagrams, so documentation no longer has to fall back to ASCII art. The diagram source stays committed markdown, which keeps it reviewable and diffable. The documentation suite plan carries the first one: the ordered docs:check -> docs:generate -> build -> deploy pipeline. Node labels are pinned to the line box mermaid measures with, because the site's wider paragraph line height otherwise pushes the last line of a multi-line label past the node border.

- Sketch extrusions now persist a separate editable source-sketch node and feature input link in `.skf` packages. Editing the profile preserves source and output identities while rebuilding the solid; undo/redo and project round-trip retain the source definition. This establishes one sketch-to-extrusion dependency.

- Sketch revolves now persist a separate editable source-sketch node and feature input link in `.skf` packages. Editing the profile rebuilds the revolve while preserving source and output identities; undo/redo and project round-trip retain the source definition. Sketch-to-extrusion and sketch-to-revolve are supported dependencies; broader downstream feature graphs remain future work.

- Sketch sweeps now persist a separate editable source-sketch node and feature input link in `.skf` packages. Editing the profile rebuilds the sweep while preserving source and output identities; undo/redo and project round-trip retain the source definition.

The output and source identities, placement, and resize ratios remain stable, while failed profiles leave the stored sweep unchanged.

- `PATCH /shapes` can also target a persisted sketch extrusion by its source ID. The engine rebuilds exact extrusion geometry from the edited profile and re-evaluates retained boolean ancestors bottom-up in one project write. Rebuilt output identities and placement are retained; invalid extrusion profiles and booleans that become ineffective leave the project unchanged.

- Small STEP assembly imports now retain their nested product names and parent/child relationships as optional `stepAssembly` metadata in project shape summaries. This remains descriptive metadata for a single combined mesh, not a set of separately editable components; extraction is bounded to 200 solids, 8 MiB, 2,000 nodes, and depth 32. The fixture under `tests/fixtures/step/` records its CadQuery provenance and regeneration path.
- Exploding a STEP assembly now carries unambiguous source occurrence ancestry onto each independently selectable exact solid as `stepComponent` metadata. The component geometry, identity, and parent path survive `.skf` project round-trips; ambiguous associations omit ancestry rather than guess. See the [STEP assembly guide](./guides/step-assemblies.md).

- The Python client can now read and use stable face identity, which it could not. `GET /faces` has reported an app-owned `id` for each face since face colours stopped being keyed by position, and the Python `FaceSummary` dropped it on the floor -- so a Python caller could see only the `index`, which the kernel reorders after any operation, and had no way to learn the id a colour has to be keyed by. `FaceSummary.id` now carries it, `faces.ids_facing(...)` answers with ids where `faces.facing(...)` answers with indexes, and a `ShapeSummary` reports `face_ids` and `face_colors` as fields rather than leaving them in `extra`. Numeric colour keys from a project saved before stable identity are kept as they are, because the server still accepts them.

  `examples/face_colors.py` is the worked example: it colours a box's top and bottom by id, rounds every roundable edge, and reports which colours came through. Run against a real server it paints 2 of 6 faces and keeps 2 of 2 through the fillet, which is the behaviour the ids exist to provide.

- The sketch constraint inspector now shows an estimated local degree-of-freedom count from the supported equations' Jacobian rank. Redundant equations are counted once; degenerate zero-length distance geometry is labeled indeterminate. The estimate includes free Bezier handle and sweep-depth parameters and does not imply a global solve.

- Sketch point drags now solve connected coincidence, horizontal/vertical, and fixed-distance constraints together with a bounded damped least-squares iteration. Dragged points stay anchored, Bezier handles translate with their points, and sweep depths remain intact. Unsatisfied constraints are still reported when an iteration cap or singular geometry prevents convergence.

- Regular STEP imports preserve available component colors on the combined mesh. The editor preview and `.skf` projects use stable face IDs for those assignments. Color extraction is limited to imports up to 200 solids and 8 MiB; ambiguous or unavailable component colors keep the shape's default color.

- Added face-based drafting for exact B-rep solids in the shape inspector. The operation uses a stable face selection and lets users choose local CAD +X, +Y, or +Z as the pull axis. The kernel rejects incompatible face, axis, and angle combinations; successful results replace the source with baked geometry. Output faces receive fresh IDs and do not inherit face colors or edge treatments. Kernel tests now verify specific +X and +Y cases on rotated exact B-rep fixtures, in addition to earlier +Z cases; see the [face-drafting guide](guides/face-drafting.md) for the measured support matrix.

- Face drafting now accepts all six signed local CAD axes, so compatible faces can be drafted while pulling along -X, -Y, or -Z as well as the positive directions. Kernel verification adds 10 mm box cases at +30° along -X and -Y; each combination remains geometry-specific and incompatible cases still surface the localized kernel error. The shape inspector's pull-axis selector lists each signed direction.

- Sweep sketching now distinguishes the closed section from the open spine with separate colors, line styles, and point markers. Its guide describes the two-stage workflow: draw the section on the selected workplane, then switch to the orthogonal plane derived from it for the spine. The generated section is oriented perpendicular to the spine's initial direction.

- The Sketch workspace can sweep multiple closed section loops along one open spine. Nested loops create through-holes; disjoint loops create separate solids. The section regions start perpendicular to the spine. Selecting a spine point lets the user set its 3D depth along the section workplane's third axis, so the spine can leave the orthogonal sketch plane; cubic Bezier handles have independent depth controls for shaping nonplanar motion between spine points. Handles without an explicit depth inherit their endpoint's depth. The first segment must remain normal to the section. The editor retains the sketch profile for reopening and editing, while generated geometry is baked rather than kept as an editable feature-history node.

- The viewport remembers its last settled camera position, target, and zoom in the project workspace, so reopening a design returns to the view you were using. Camera movement is saved after orbiting, panning, or zooming settles; older projects without a saved view still open at the usual home view.

- Sketch profiles can carry coincident, horizontal, vertical, and fixed-distance constraints. The Sketch workspace can add or remove these constraints from selected points or segments, and dragging geometry projects it back toward its constraints while keeping dragged points anchored. Unsatisfied constraint IDs appear in the editor notice, and `.skf` saves the declarations while continuing to open older unconstrained profiles. This is a local projection solver; sketches still bake into shapes and do not participate in a parametric feature tree.

- `GET /faces` now returns stable application-owned face IDs alongside compatibility indexes. Face colors persist by ID, render on STEP and other B-rep-backed shapes, and export to 3MF per triangle. Unambiguous unchanged faces keep their assignments through CAD treatments; generated or ambiguous faces receive new IDs without inherited colors. Numeric keys remain supported for older projects and callers.
- Exploding a retained STEP assembly now reads its XCAF product labels before splitting the solids. Named products keep their STEP names, and explicit or inherited component colors are used when the STEP reader supplies them; unlabeled solids keep the existing numbered names and imported shape color. Labels are matched to solids by exact volume and bounds, so component ordering does not decide which label lands on which part.
- Added `POST /api/v1/draft-prism` for retained straight sketch extrusions. The server rebuilds a closed polygon profile with OCCT's draft-prism operation, then stores both the exact BREP and viewport mesh. The TypeScript and Python clients expose it. This first slice requires a single closed linear loop and does not store an editable feature-tree node.

- Added a draft-prism control to the shape inspector for retained straight sketch extrusions. It accepts angles between -45° and 45°, excluding zero, and uses the local CAD worker to replace the selection with exact BREP and mesh geometry. This operation is baked and does not add an editable feature-tree node.

- Added a face-draft operation for exact B-rep shapes in the shape inspector. It addresses faces through their stable IDs, uses the local CAD +Z pull axis, and replaces the source with baked geometry and fresh face IDs. The result clears prior face colors and edge-treatment metadata. Unsupported face/angle combinations show a localized kernel error. Its measured support is limited: the editor's sketch extrusion fixture supports faces 1, 3, 4, and 5 at ±5°; a 10 mm box supports faces 0–3 at ±5°.

- Align, distribute, repeat and import are now operations rather than buttons. `POST /api/v1/align`, `/distribute`, `/patterns` and `/imports` join the API, and all four are on the `Engine`, which means a macro with no server and no browser can reach them too. Align and distribute were editor-only because their arithmetic lived inside `SketchForgeEditor.tsx` with the overlay that drives them; repeat and import were editor-only because nothing had asked for them anywhere else. A script could place a row of bosses to four decimal places and could not ask for their tops to be flush, which is a strange shape for a capability to have.

  The MCP bridge has offered all four since it was written, by driving a live editor tab. It is still doing that; this is the half of the work that has to exist before it can stop.

- The `Engine` facade also gained the operations the `shapes` route had kept to itself: adding shapes from parameters, changing one, removing one, and reading a project's shapes with its name, revision and units. #63 left that route alone on purpose, because it is not the lock-read-mutate-write shape the other seven share -- a batch of two hundred shapes is built *before* the lock is taken, since sweeping a twenty-turn thread takes longer than writing the project and there is no reason to hold it busy for that. The route now does what the other seven do: parse the request, pick a status code, serialise the reply. It lost about sixty lines and gained nothing; the thread catalog, the revolve rebuild and the per-kind parameter table moved to where a macro can reach them.

  Every refusal keeps its exact wording and status, including the three different sentences `unapplicable_fields` can carry -- a parameter the kind is not built from, a sweep field on something with no sweep, and a sweep whose outline was drawn rather than described. `tests/unit/shapeRequestParity.test.ts` and the shape round-trip suite pin the `PATCH` contract #40 and #60 established and both still pass untouched.

- A pattern's own refusals are worth having rather than silently doing nothing. A row with no step and a ring with a zero sweep would both put every copy exactly on top of the original, and a `201` saying it created shapes you cannot see is the one reply a script cannot tell from success. Both are `422` and nothing is written. The same reasoning runs the other way for an alignment that finds everything already flush: that reports `moved: 0` and writes nothing at all, because bumping the revision would push an undo entry and tell every open dashboard to reload in order to say that nothing happened.

- Importing over the API keeps the source file, which is the half that is easy to leave out. An imported shape carries its triangles *and* an id pointing at the file it came from, and the file lives on the project as an asset -- write the shape without the asset and the next save strips the geometry back out. Both land in one write. STEP is deliberately not here, and reading one is no longer the obstacle -- the server-side STEP importer added alongside this work would manage it. Writing one is: a shape whose mesh came from a source asset is re-imported from that asset when the project is read back, and that path loads the browser's kernel, so a STEP import written by the server would be write-only and the next read of the project would fail. Fixing it means handing the `.skf` reader a server-side source importer, which would also fix reading a project whose STEP import was made in the browser -- a bug that is already there.

- `distributeShapes.ts` and `patternShapes.ts` moved under `lib/engine/`, where the boundary test now covers them, and a new `alignShapes.ts` joins them. All three are pure arithmetic over extents rather than over shapes, so the editor can keep feeding them mesh bounds while the engine feeds them the stored footprint. That difference is real and documented: the editor measures a rotated box's swept footprint and the engine measures the width you sent it, which is the number a caller can predict.

- Both client libraries carry the four endpoints with the server's own field names, and the error table gained `unsupported_format` (415) for a model file nothing here can read -- distinguished from `geometry_failed` (422), which is a format we do read and a file we could not parse. Only one of the two is worth retrying with the same file.

- Fixed importing a real STEP assembly, which failed outright. A 74 MB FreeCAD export of a Raspberry Pi 5 board -- 45,689 faces in 2,689 solids -- read and rotated in 22 seconds and then died with `memory access out of bounds`, a raw WebAssembly trap with no message a person could act on. It was not a memory ceiling: the kernel grows to 4 GB, the failure was identical under Node, and coarsening the tessellation from 0.1 to 1.0 did not move it. The cause was the order of operations -- the import rotated the solid from Z-up to Y-up *before* meshing it, and rotating copies the whole B-rep, so the kernel held two 74 MB models at the moment it needed room to tessellate.

  There is now a server-side import that meshes first and turns the triangles instead, which is the same -90 degrees about X for the cost of a swap per vertex rather than a copy of the model. The same file now imports in 27 seconds as 730,598 triangles measuring 88.50 x 18.98 x 57.63 mm. The exact geometry is deliberately left out above 200 solids or 8 MB of source, because re-exporting that assembly produces 202.7 MB of STEP text and the project is written on every autosave; the mesh is unaffected, and the result says whether exact geometry was kept so a caller can explain a later fillet. In the browser, where only the old mesher is available, the failure now arrives as a sentence saying the file needs a server rather than as a WebAssembly trap.

- Pointed everything that names a repository at this one. Forking left several references on the project this was forked from, and because nothing asserted them, nothing complained. Three of them were not cosmetic: the desktop updater's release feed asked *upstream* what the newest version was, and upstream's newest is older than ours, so every desktop build we ship had an updater that could never offer an update; the self-hosted "update" button compared against upstream's `package.json` and would have pulled upstream's code over this one, replacing every feature here; and the source-code link offered upstream's source, where the AGPL expects a network user to be offered the corresponding source of the build actually running. The container image paths in the README, the REST API guide and the ghcr compose file pointed at upstream's image too, so a reader following our own instructions ran a build with none of this work in it.

  A test now asserts each of them, including that the attribution still points at the original author -- repointing what is functional should not quietly erase credit. The sponsor link and the upstream issue references stay as they are, because they are correct.

- The geometry engine now lives in one directory, `apps/web/src/lib/engine/`, with a boundary a test enforces rather than a convention a reviewer has to remember. Forty modules moved there -- the `.skf` format, the exact OCCT builders, the shape-from-parameters table, the importers, the placement and grouping maths, the mesh primitives -- and nothing under that directory may reach `@/i18n`, `@/components`, `react` or `next`. The guard walks the whole transitive `@/` closure including type-only imports, because a type-only import is erased from the bundle but still decides whether a module can be moved, and that is exactly how `@/i18n` got into the server's graph the last time.

  What this unblocks is the thing a macro cannot do today. A script that builds a part offline has no server to call, so it cannot group, cut, fillet or hollow anything -- and it writes a project directory without taking the project lock, so two macros, or a macro and an open dashboard, can lose each other's work. The orchestration those operations need was copied into each of the seven REST handlers and reachable only over HTTP; it is now an `Engine` bound to a project store, which means a macro runner gets the lock, the revision bump and the undo entry by constructing one. The directory is also what a package is made from: lifting it out of the app becomes a path change rather than an archaeology exercise.

  The twelve places where engine code still reaches back into the app are listed in the guard with a reason each, so the residue is written down rather than rediscovered. Two of them are the ones to cut first: the STEP exporter still loads the browser's OCCT kernel itself, where the design says a kernel should be handed to it, and the project settings normalizer reaches a module that reaches the theme code, which touches `document`.

- `serverCadSolids.ts` is now `cadSolids.ts`. The `server` prefix has been wrong since the editor's cuts moved onto the exact kernel and started building their operands with it in the browser; the module's own docstring said so. Nothing else was renamed, because nothing else has been checked.

- The seven REST handlers that do geometry -- `booleans`, `groups`, `parts`, `edges`, `faces`, `treatments`, `shell` -- now do HTTP and nothing else: parse the request, pick a status code, name an error code, serialise the reply. Between them they lost a third of their lines, and `booleans` and `groups` lost the duplicate: grouping and cutting are the same operation with one difference, whether the hole marks come off the stored shapes or out of the request, and they are now the same code. No response changed, down to the wording of every refusal.

- CAD geometry can now be measured, from the exact B-rep rather than from the triangles. `GET /api/v1/measurements` reports every edge and face of a shape -- an edge's length along its own curve, the radius and diameter of a circular edge or arc, a face's real surface area, and a cylindrical face's radius -- and `POST /api/v1/measurements` answers the minimum distance between any two entities, which may be two whole solids, two named faces, or an edge of one shape against a face of another, plus the angle between them when both are flat. Both client libraries carry it and the MCP bridge gained `sketchforge_measure_shape` and `sketchforge_measure_between`. This answers upstream #113, which asked for "the length of an edge, distance between edges/faces, radius/diameter of circles/arches".

  Exact, not approximate, is the whole of the feature rather than a detail of it. A tessellated cylinder has no radius at all -- only facets -- so a measurement read off the mesh is out by the chord error of whatever quality setting was in force, and wrong in precisely the cases somebody reaches for a measuring tool. A circular edge is measured as its arc length over the angle it sweeps, which is exact for a full circle and for an arc alike, so a bore reads 7.5mm at any tessellation quality. The same class of quiet error is what #57 found in a sphere that measured 0.0427% off its true volume.

- A measurement that is not meaningful for the entity selected is refused with a reason rather than guessed at, which is most of the design. An elliptical edge has two semi-axes and no single radius, and `length / sweep` on one would report something between them: a plausible number, wrong, and impossible to notice. A cone's radius changes along it, so whatever point was sampled would be reported as *the* radius of the face. A torus has a tube radius and a ring radius with no way to say which was meant. Each of those comes back as `null` with a code -- `ellipseHasNoSingleRadius`, `radiusVariesOverSurface` -- beside the measurements that are exact, and the documentation has the table.

- A spherical face is refused too, and the reason is worth recording because the obvious implementation is wrong. `surfaceCurvature` looks like the way to get a radius: a sphere's principal curvatures are both `1/r`, so `1 / max` ought to be it. Measured on a sphere of radius 13 and a cylinder of radius 7.5, the four fields that call returns do not match their names consistently between the two. On the sphere `min` is -1/13, which is a principal curvature, while `max` is 1/169, which is the Gaussian; on the cylinder `min` is -1/15, which is the mean, while `mean` is -1/7.5, which is the principal. A radius derived from any single field is therefore right for one of those surfaces and exactly a factor of two wrong for the other. So the cylinder's radius comes from `getFaceCylinderData`, the kernel's own cylinder accessor, which measures 7.5 for a 7.5mm cylinder; the sphere is reported as `radiusNotReportedByKernel`, which says the geometry has a radius and this kernel will not hand it over, and the circular edge bounding it still measures exactly.

- A distance is measured where the shapes actually sit, which took finding. The edge and face listings build a shape with `orientedSolidForShape`, which applies the rotation and not the translation -- correct for them, since a length and an area do not depend on where a part is. A distance is the one measurement that does, and through that same builder two parts 60mm apart both measured from the origin and read zero. It now goes through the placement a boolean between the same two shapes would use, so the rotation is applied exactly once and a mirrored shape still reflects. A unit test pins two 40mm boxes whose centres are 60mm apart at the 20mm between their facing walls, and the e2e suite asks a real server the same question about a round post and a plate.

- The maths is in `lib/cadMeasurements.ts` with no kernel in it, and every assertion is a closed form rather than a figure read back and pasted in: a circumference of `2pi(7.5)`, a cone's refusal, `pi(13)` for the half-circle seam of a sphere, `acos(1/sqrt(3))` for an oblique angle between two normals. The fixtures are deliberately off the origin and deliberately not square -- a 40x25 plate tells its own two in-plane edge lengths apart, which a 40x40 one cannot, and a radius of 1 would make `length / sweep`, `length * sweep` and `1 / sweep` all agree.

- Fixed `POST /booleans` and `POST /groups` answering `201` with a group that is really just the unchanged solid, when none of the holes met it. The kernel's `cutAll` hands the solid back untouched in that case and says nothing about having done so, and this path never compared the volume before and after -- so a hole placed at the wrong coordinates was stored and reported as a successful cut. A script has no picture to check that against, which makes it worse here than the same mistake would be in the editor, where the result is visible. The editor's own boolean has compared those two volumes since it moved to the exact kernel -- its comment reads "the mesh paths treat that as a failed cut rather than a group, and so does this" -- and the server now uses the same comparison and the same threshold, so the two agree about what counts as a cut. The measurement is reported by the geometry layer and refused by the two routes, before anything is written, which keeps a hole that deliberately misses usable for observing where a placed solid lands -- the technique the rotation regression test uses.

- The server also now asks whether a cut's result is geometry the kernel considers valid, before storing it. It is stored in `cadBrep`, which a later fillet or a STEP export reads, so an invalid result was a failure deferred to whoever opened the project next. The check deliberately accepts a compound rather than demanding a single solid, because a cut can legitimately split one body into several -- which is what `POST /parts` exists to separate -- and a test pins that, since requiring a single solid is the obvious "tidy-up" that would refuse real results.

- Fixed `POST /shapes` dropping parameters it documents as applicable. A request for a tapered box -- `{"kind": "box", "taperTopWidth": 2}` -- came back as a straight box with a `201`, because the shape was built by a branch per kind that named only the parameters that kind cannot be built without, and no branch mentioned taper. `PATCH` applied the same fields all along by looping over the shared parameter table, so creating a tapered box and patching one produced different shapes, and the only way to discover it was to create one and then patch it. Create now reads the same table, so a parameter added to it works on both paths without anyone remembering to touch a branch. A kind's own default still wins where it has one -- an unreadable `sides` on a pyramid is 4, not the table's generic 96 -- and a parameter the kind does not claim is still not applied, which is the direction `PATCH` refuses with a 422.

- A sphere, a dome, a torus and a tapered cone can now be hollowed. `POST /api/v1/shell` refused all of them, and the reason given -- that OCCT will not offset a continuous curved surface -- was wrong. OCCT offsets an analytic sphere, cylinder, cone and torus perfectly well; what it refuses is a **spline** skin. The sphere was refused because this app built one as a revolved spline even when all three radii matched, so the most obvious hollow part anybody would ask for -- a ball -- was turned away by a limit it did not have. A ball of equal radii is now built as a true sphere and hollows to the volume the arithmetic says: 40mm across with a 2mm wall is 4/3 pi (20^3 - 18^3) = 9081.2972mm^3, exactly, centred exactly on its own centre.

- That also makes a sphere's volume right everywhere else it is used. OCCT's ellipsoid is a revolved spline whose volume is a fraction of a percent out -- 4187.0 against 4188.79 for a 20mm ball -- and a boolean cut was cutting with that approximation. An elliptical ball is still a spline, still approximate, and still cannot be hollowed; only the equal-radii case can be analytic.

- The documented list of what hollows is now the measured one, with the real reason beside it. A pointed cone cannot be hollowed because offsetting inwards from an apex has nowhere to go -- give it a `topRadius` and it works. A `tube`, `ring` or `roundRoof` cannot, because their walls are built by extruding a profile, which is a spline surface however round it looks. A `loft` cannot, for the same reason. Each of those answers `422` saying which.

- The dark highlight that shows where a hole will land now agrees with the cut it is previewing, and there is one place that decides what agreeing means. This app had three allowances on a hole -- 0.05mm on the mesh cut, 0.01mm on the preview, and nothing at all on the exact cut that now commits -- spread over two files with no stated relationship between them, so nobody could say what the highlight was promising. They are all in `cutterPadding` now, in a table, with the one rule each obeys: the preview shows the hole as drawn, which is what the exact cut removes, grown only by a separation its own evaluator needs. It could instead have mirrored whichever backend was about to run, and shown the mesh path's 0.05mm where that one serves -- but nobody can see which backend ran, and that overcut is a defect of the fallback rather than anything anyone asked for.

- That separation is not negotiable, and the measurement is worth recording because the comment that used to justify it understated the problem. On a 40x40x10 plate with a 12mm blind pocket whose floor is flush with the plate's top face, the preview's `HOLLOW_INTERSECTION` returns 179 triangles in 11.6ms with the 0.01mm separation, and had not returned after four minutes with none. Coplanar faces fed to that evaluator do not merely multiply the triangle count, as was thought; they stop it coming back.

- Fixed the preview under-separating a tapered hole, which is how dragging one over a solid could hang the editor. The highlight grows the hole by scaling its drawn mesh, and a scale grows a narrow section by less than a wide one: a cutter tapering from 20mm to 2mm, scaled to clear a hundredth of a millimetre at its mouth, cleared a thousandth at its tip -- which is the condition above. The growth is now scaled by the taper's own ratio, so the narrowest section clears the full separation, and capped at the 0.05mm this app already treats as an acceptable allowance on a cut, so a cutter tapering to nothing cannot grow the highlight without bound.

- The preview is deliberately still a mesh operation rather than an exact one, and that was measured rather than assumed. An exact cut of a single round pocket takes 3.6ms of kernel time, 8.5ms for a tapered one and 65ms for a plate with three holes, and it would have to go through the CAD worker and wait on a 22MB kernel load the first time -- against a highlight redrawn on every pointer movement. A preview that is right and late is worse than one that is right to a hundredth of a millimetre and immediate. What #48 left behind was three uncentralised constants and an untested relationship, not a wrong number: before it, the preview showed 0.01mm where the mesh cut removed 0.05mm, so it was four times further from the truth than it is now.

- The editor's mesh primitives are no longer built twice. Seven of them -- the roof, the wedge, the pyramid, the barrel roof, the dome, the torus and the tube -- existed as one copy in `WorkplaneViewport.tsx` for the thing on screen and a second, near-verbatim copy in `SketchForgeEditor.tsx` under `createBoolean*` names for the operands of a mesh boolean. The vertex arrays, the index arrays and the attribute setup were byte-identical; what differed was the last statement, and in one case the shading. Both now come from `lib/primitiveGeometry.ts`, with the two real differences preserved as options rather than reconciled: `indexed` for the boolean path's index buffer, and `halfSphereNormals` for the dome's analytic-or-averaged normals. Nothing about what gets drawn or cut has changed, and that was checked rather than assumed -- both old copies were extracted and compared attribute by attribute against the new module across five sets of dimensions and every tessellation parameter they take, 175 comparisons, all identical.

  The reason this is worth a changelog entry is not the line count. A copied builder is invisible until the copies disagree, and these two had already begun to: the viewport computed analytic ellipsoid normals for the dome with a dedicated flat normal across its bottom cap, while the boolean copy averaged face normals instead, so the same dome shaded one way on screen and fed different normals to the CSG evaluator. Nobody could have written the test that would notice, because the test harness has no JSX plugin and no DOM environment -- nothing in a `.tsx` file is reachable from a test at all, which is around twenty thousand lines of this application including every mesh primitive and all three mesh-boolean backends. Moving a builder into a `.ts` module is what makes it testable, and this is the first of those moves.

- There are now tests that can see a primitive builder, and a test that compares the mesh ones against the exact ones. Each builder is measured against an independently derived closed form -- a prism's centroid a third of the way up its triangle, a pyramid's a quarter, a half-ellipsoid's three-eighths, a half-ellipse's `4b/3pi` -- and each tessellated kind is also asserted to come in *under* the smooth volume, since an inscribed polygon must. Separately, every kind that both the mesh dispatch and the exact OCCT dispatch in `serverCadSolids.ts` can build -- nine of them, counting a hexagonal pyramid and a tube and a ring -- is now built both ways and compared.

  Volume and centre of mass, never bounding extents. A bounding box cannot see a mirrored solid or a reversed winding, and a box or a cylinder is exactly invariant under the 180-degree error a negated yaw produces; that blind spot has already hidden two real bugs here, a box built from its corner instead of its centre and a rotation composition that disagreed with the editor in four of six cases while every test in the suite passed either way. The signed volume doubles as a winding check, since a mesh wound inside out is a hole as far as a CSG evaluator is concerned and is identical to the correct one by every extent you could measure.

  The three dispatches turn out to agree, within tessellation, on all nine kinds -- so this found nothing today. It is there for the day one of them is edited and the others are not, which is the day that has already cost this project three shipped bugs.

- Fixed server-side CAD geometry exporting to STEP at the origin instead of where the shape is. A fillet, chamfer or hollow done on the server stores its exact geometry in `cadBrep` with a `cadBrepFrame` saying where that geometry sits, and both things that read it -- STEP export and the editor's own CAD worker -- place the solid by subtracting the frame's centre and adding the shape's current one. The server recorded the *shape's* placement in that frame for geometry that is actually in the local frame every builder here produces, so the placement subtracted itself out: a part at x=25, z=-15, elevation=7 came back at 0, 0, 0. The frame now records the result's own bounds, which is what the editor's worker has always written for the same reason. Nothing caught this because a shape left where it was created subtracts zero, and because a misplaced part has exactly the size it should.

- Fixed `GET /api/v1/edges` answering `404 {"code":"api_disabled"}` from a production build, whatever token the server was started with. A route handler that exports only GET and touches no API Next recognises as dynamic is rendered at build time and then served from the full route cache; the build has no `SKETCHFORGE_API_TOKEN`, so the cached answer is the one that says the API is switched off. Every other route in `/api/v1` escaped by accident, by exporting POST or DELETE as well. Both read-only routes now export OPTIONS -- which is a true thing for an API to answer and, more to the point, stops the prerender, because the static-export build refuses every way of *declaring* a GET-only route dynamic. `tests/e2e/productionApi.e2e.ts` now builds and starts a real server, which is the only kind of test that can see this.

- The end-to-end suite's dev server now builds into `.next-dev` as the config intends. It inherited `NODE_ENV=test` from vitest, and the config only separates the dev compiler from the production one when NODE_ENV says development -- so two suites in the same run fought over `.next`, one failing its build and the other dying with "Cannot find module for page: /_document".

- Fixed a boolean cut turning a rotated shape twice. `POST /api/v1/booleans` builds each operand as an exact solid and then puts it where the shape sits with `shapePlacementMatrix`, which carries the rotation -- but the builder it called, `localSolidForShape`, baked the rotation in as well, so a rotated parametric operand was turned twice by the same rotation. A 20x20x12 wedge at yaw 90 was cut 3.33mm out of place in each of X and Z. #45 fixed *how* the two compositions compose and left them both in the call path, which made this cleaner rather than fixed.

- Nothing could have caught it. The volume is a rigid-motion invariant, a wedge's footprint is square so every extent was identical, and `meshYawDegrees` reduces a circular cylinder's yaw modulo its own side step -- so the shape most people would have tried was genuinely unaffected. `grep -rn "buildGroupFromSelection" tests/` also returned nothing: the server's boolean entry point had no test at all. It now has `tests/e2e/booleanRotation.e2e.ts`, which measures centre of mass against the editor's own placement composition.

- The two intents are now separate functions rather than a flag between them. `localSolidForShape` returns the shape's own unrotated frame, which is what its name and its docstring always said and what a caller holding a placement matrix needs; `orientedSolidForShape` is that plus the rotation, which is what a fillet, chamfer or hollow needs, because those store their result as a mesh with no rotation of its own. Both read one predicate, so they cannot drift apart again. The edge listing moved to the oriented solid too: an index is only a name for an edge if the listing and the treatment are looking at the same solid, and a turned shape's edge midpoints were being reported square.

- The REST API can now hollow a solid out. `POST /shell` offsets every face of a shape inwards by a wall thickness and takes the result away from the original, optionally leaving named faces off -- so a box becomes a shell, and a box with its top open becomes a tray. This is the biggest thing missing from a tool aimed at printing: a solid 20mm cube costs four times the filament of the same cube with a 2mm wall, and until now the only way to get one was to model the cavity by hand as a second shape and subtract it, which gives a constant wall only if you work the arithmetic out yourself. The operation is exact OCCT, and the result is stored the way a fillet's is -- a mesh to draw with the true geometry alongside it -- so a hollowed part still exports to STEP as real surfaces and can still be filleted afterwards.

- Faces to leave open are named by direction -- `top`, `bottom`, `left`, `right`, `front`, `back` -- or by index from the new `GET /faces`, which lists each face's area, surface type, centroid and outward normal. A direction means the outermost flat face pointing that way and only that one, which matters on a part that is already hollow: the floor of its cavity points up as surely as its lid does, and opening the floor rather than the lid looks exactly like nothing happening.

- A wall too thick for the part is refused with the project untouched. OCCT does not report that as an error: past the thickness that fits it variously returns an empty compound, a solid that fails its own validity check while reporting the correct bounding box, or -- worst of the three -- the original solid untouched, valid and the right size, having hollowed nothing. None of those can be told from a real answer by its extents, so the server weighs the result instead; hollowing always removes the cavity, and a result that weighs what it started at did nothing.

- Not every shape can be hollowed, and the refusal says which: a sphere, a torus, a cone, a tube's round wall and a loft each present one continuous curved surface with no separate faces to offset inwards, and the kernel refuses them at every thickness and at every reconstruction tolerance it offers. Boxes, cylinders and prisms, polygons, pyramids, roofs and wedges all work. For the rest, cut the cavity out with `POST /booleans` instead -- a different result, but one that works on a sphere.

The MCP bridge gained `sketchforge_hollow_shape` and `sketchforge_list_faces`, which drive the REST API against a stored project rather than an open editor tab -- hollowing is a server-side operation and there is no editor action for it yet.

- The loft and the gear are each now two modules rather than one, split along the line between the maths and the mesh: `loftProfile` and `gearProfile` hold the normalizers and the profile geometry and import no `three`, while `loftMesh` and `gearMesh` hold the `BufferGeometry` builders. `loftGeometry` and `gearGeometry` re-export both halves, so nothing in the editor moved. The server-side CAD builder needs the normalizers -- it builds a loft as an exact solid and has to settle the same questions about profile shape, rotation and top size that the drawn one does -- and because they shared a file with a mesh builder, asking for them dragged the renderer and a React-adjacent `@/i18n` type import into the server's dependency graph alongside them. It no longer does, which is a step towards an engine that can be lifted out of the app and reused by the MCP bridge and the client libraries without carrying a renderer. There is a test asserting that boundary, in the same shape as the one guarding `@/i18n` against import errors, so it cannot quietly come back.

- The normalizers both halves agree on are now tested directly rather than inferred from the vertices of a finished mesh, which is what made the split worth doing beyond tidiness. Two things came out of writing those tests. A polygon loft end is centred on its true circumcenter, and there is now an area-and-centroid assertion saying so, because a bounding box cannot see a profile that has been quietly recentred -- the same blind spot that hid a mirrored solid and an off-centre box. And a gear's root-circle floor, the 34% of the outer radius a bore may never exceed, turns out to be unreachable at any ordinary size: the tooth size is capped at 22% of the smaller dimension before the root radius is taken, which always leaves at least 56%, so the floor only binds on a gear under about a millimetre across. Both branches are now pinned, so whichever one a future change means to move, it will have to say so.

- Fixed server-side CAD turning a shape the wrong way, so a fillet, chamfer or STEP export of a shape that had been rotated came back facing somewhere else. The editor orients a shape with `THREE.Euler(rotationX, rotation, rotationZ)` in its default `XYZ` order -- the matrix `Rx * Ry * Rz` -- while the exact-solid builder negated the yaw and applied the three rotations in the opposite order. Of seven representative rotations, four disagreed with the editor: yaw alone came out mirrored, and every combination of two or more came out in the wrong order. Only single-axis pitch and roll were right.

  Nothing caught it because the shapes people fillet while turned are usually symmetric: a box, a cylinder or a sphere is unchanged by the 180-degree error a negated yaw produces, so the bug was invisible until an asymmetric shape -- a wedge, a roof, a cone with a top radius, a pyramid -- was rotated and then treated. There is now a test that measures the solid's centre of mass, which is the part a wrong orientation moves, against the editor's own matrix for all four of those kinds; a bounding box cannot see this, since mirroring a solid does not change its extents.

It now sits with the other workspace settings, travels in the `.skf` project file, and goes through the same normalizer as everything else there: a project saved before the setting existed, or carrying a value this build has never heard of, opens in perspective instead of leaving the viewport asking for a camera it cannot build. The toggle and its `O` shortcut are where they were. Switching still frames the same scene from the same direction -- the orthographic frustum is built as tall as the perspective camera already saw at the orbit target, and coming back the camera is placed at the distance that sees the frustum the orthographic camera was showing, zoom included. That last part matters because zoom means two different things either side of the switch: an orthographic camera zooms by shrinking its frustum, since sliding it along its own axis changes nothing at all on screen. The conversions now live in `lib/cameraProjection.ts`, where they are covered by tests, rather than inline in the viewport.

- Fixed the snap grid being local to each shape rather than global to the world, which is upstream issue #117. It was local in two ways at once. The lattice was anchored on the near edge of the workplane -- `-width/2` on X, `-depth/2` on Z -- so a plate whose span was not a whole number of grid blocks snapped onto lines running through neither the world origin nor the lines a pointer drag or an arrow key snap to, the X and Z lattices could be offset differently from each other because width and depth are set separately, and resizing the plate slid the whole grid sideways under the model. The drawn grid had the same anchor, so on a 203mm plate there was no axis line through the origin to read at all, and the major line every fifth block moved when the plate was resized. Both now run through the world origin at multiples of the block size. The reference point was local too: whichever of the footprint's two edges happened to lie nearest a line was the one placed on it, and which edge that is depends on the shape's size -- so two shapes of different sizes came to rest on two different lattices and would not line up with each other, which is the symptom that was reported. The near corner of the footprint now always decides it, moved to its nearest line, so a snap still never shifts a shape by more than half a block. For a rotated shape that corner is the corner of its rotated bounding box, and for a shape whose footprint is not centred on its origin it is the footprint's corner and not the origin, so what lands on the grid is the thing you can see.

- Designs now export as 3MF, which is what a modern slicer would rather be given than an STL. The STL writer throws away two things the slicer then has to guess at: the unit, so a design is a pile of unitless numbers that happens to be read as millimetres, and the colour, along with the boundary between one shape and the next -- a selection of five parts arrives as one anonymous triangle soup. A 3MF is an OPC package (`[Content_Types].xml`, `_rels/.rels`, `3D/3dmodel.model`) whose model declares `unit="millimeter"` and carries one named `<object>` per shape, each with its own mesh, plus a `<build>` placing each object with its own transform. Each object is written in its own frame -- centred on its footprint and resting on z = 0 -- and the build item carries the placement, which is the shape a slicer expects a part to arrive in. The archive pins its ZIP timestamps the same way the `.skf` writer does, so exporting the same design twice gives the same bytes.

The extension would have to be declared in `requiredextensions`, and a reader that does not implement it is then required to reject the whole file; `displaycolor` is part of the core, so a reader that ignores colour still gets correct geometry. - A shape can now be given a material -- PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, TPU, nylon, polycarbonate or resin -- and the inspector shows what the part would weigh and what it would cost. Each preset's density is the figure from the manufacturers' data sheets, in g/cm3, cited in `lib/materialPresets.ts`; the price per kilogram is a setting with a sensible default per material, editable next to the readout and saved with the project, since nobody's filament costs what a hardcoded number says. A hole has no weight of its own and says so instead of pricing the material it removes. The weight is for a solid part, which is the upper bound -- a sparse infill prints lighter.

- The editor's boolean cuts are now exact, which fixes upstream issues #118 ("combining a negative space cylinder with a block results in a broken mesh") and #111 ("Tapered Box combined with other object results in square not tapered hole"). An OCCT call that throws leaves the kernel in a state its own API cannot repair, so a self-hosted server would answer "null function or function signature mismatch" to everything until restarted, after a single fillet radius that did not fit. The kernel is now discarded and rebuilt after a failure, as the editor's worker already does.
- Server-side fillet and chamfer now work on every parametric kind the server can build exactly, not just boxes: cylinders (round, faceted, oval, and tapered), cones, spheres, half spheres, lofts, polygon prisms, tubes and rings. Each is rebuilt as true geometry -- a round cylinder is a cylinder, so a fillet lands on a real cylindrical surface rather than on a 96-sided approximation of one. Kinds whose geometry is font- or tessellation-derived still report that they need the editor.
- Fixed a shape losing its rotation when filleted through the REST API. The geometry is now built turned the way the shape sits, so a treated shape keeps its angle instead of springing back to square.
- The REST API can now round and cut edges on the server: `GET .../shapes/{id}/edges` lists a shape's edges and `POST .../shapes/{id}/treatments` applies a fillet or chamfer. The editor does this in a browser worker, so every client downloads the 22 MB CAD kernel and runs the solid modelling itself; a self-hosted server can now do that work instead. A box is rebuilt from its dimensions so its fillet is exact rather than a rounding of a tessellation, and the result stores exact geometry so STEP export and later treatments build on true surfaces. A radius that does not fit the shape is reported rather than approximated.
- Added a REST API for scripting projects, enabled with `SKETCHFORGE_API_TOKEN` alongside server-side storage. It creates projects and adds, changes, and deletes shapes described as JSON, with no editor open, so an LLM or a build script can generate a parameterised design instead of placing each shape by hand. Shapes it cannot build from parameters alone are reported as unsupported rather than substituted. See `docs/REST_API.md`.

The dashboard reads its list from the server and autosaves there, projects already in a browser are moved up on first run, and a browser whose projects cannot all be moved keeps its own storage rather than showing half a list.

Negative and fractional values are accepted, and rotation reads as a signed turn so a shape stored at 350 degrees shows as -10.

- Import and Export needed a window 1228px wide before the toolbar was fully usable, which is the complaint in #87 almost exactly -- "far above 1200px". The tools ribbon wanted that much at its natural size, and what happened below it was worse than cramped: the sections were allowed to shrink under their own buttons down to an 88px floor, so a five-button section squeezed to 88px drew its buttons spilling over its neighbours, where the next section's background painted over them. Narrower than 864px the row stopped fitting at all and the rightmost controls, Manage among them, were pushed past the edge of a bar that does not scroll -- except below 980px, where a media query turned scrolling on, so a 900px window could reach buttons a 950px window could not. The three Manage actions now sit in the title row beside the project name, which is where the report asked for them and where nothing else competes for the width, and they are pinned to its right edge: at any width down to 343px, Import, Export and workspace settings are on screen. Sections no longer shrink under their buttons, and the ribbon scrolls at every width rather than only below 980px, so a narrow window gets a ribbon you can scroll instead of icons hidden behind each other.

- The ribbon also has a second, smaller size now, which is the other half of what was asked for. Below the width its full-size layout needs, buttons go from 43px to 37px and section labels from 12px to 10px -- every control still present, in the same place, on a tighter grid -- and that buys 184px, enough that the whole geometry ribbon fits a 900px window without scrolling, where before it wanted 1228px to lay itself out honestly. 37px is the floor rather than a taste: the toolbar artwork is drawn at 35px, and a smaller button would be rescaling it.

  Which size applies at which width lives in `lib/toolbarLayout.ts` rather than in a media query, and it is derived rather than chosen -- a section is as wide as its buttons or its minimum, the ribbon is its sections plus the rules between them, and the two widths that falls out of (1081px and 897px) are what the component switches on. A breakpoint written in CSS is a number no test can read; these are tested either side of the step, and so is the decision to keep spelling out "Import" and "Export" above 461px and show bare icons below it.
